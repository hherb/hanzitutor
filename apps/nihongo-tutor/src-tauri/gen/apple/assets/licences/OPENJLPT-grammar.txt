OpenJLPT — the grammar course
=============================

The grammar patterns this app teaches, the explanation of what each one means, the
form it is built from, and the example sentences that show it are the work of
**OpenJLPT** and its contributors. Credit for them belongs to OpenJLPT and to the
people who wrote them; this project claims no copyright over that material.

Attribution, as the licence asks it to be given
-----------------------------------------------

  Contains grammar data from OpenJLPT (https://github.com/evanclan/OpenJLPT),
  released under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International
  Licence (CC BY-SA 4.0).

  The grammar points — the explanations and every example sentence — are original
  writing by OpenJLPT's contributors. OpenJLPT's own `NOTICE.md` states it in as
  many words: the dataset is assembled from the open sources below **"plus original
  content written for the project (the grammar dataset and the corrections file)"**,
  and the project's `sources/grammar/README.md` adds that the explanations and all
  example sentences were written for OpenJLPT rather than copied from a textbook or
  a website. `prepare-grammar` **refuses to build** if `NOTICE.md` stops saying that,
  because it is the basis for taking this dataset and nothing else from the
  repository.

  The same `NOTICE.md` names the sources its *other* data draws on — Jonathan
  Waller's JLPT lists, EDRDG's JMdict and KANJIDIC2, Tatoeba's sentences and
  KanjiVG's diagrams — none of which this app takes from here.

What this app takes, and what it does not
-----------------------------------------

  **Only the grammar dataset.** This app does not take OpenJLPT's vocabulary,
  its kanji, its vocabulary levels, its kanji levels, or the example sentences
  attached to its vocabulary entries: the vocabulary and the kanji come from EDRDG
  directly (see the EDRDG notice beside this one), and the graded sentences on the
  Phrases screen come from Tatoeba directly (see the Tatoeba notice). Waller's lists
  are not used anywhere in this project, and neither is KanjiVG's diagram set.

  The example sentences this app **does** take are the grammar dataset's own — the
  two or three written for each grammar point — which are OpenJLPT's contributors'
  work and are attributed above.

  That matters, and it is the reason the grammar half is usable where the rest of
  that dataset is not: the level assignments OpenJLPT carries rest on an
  unversioned community grant that this project does not consider verified, while
  the grammar explanations and examples are OpenJLPT's own writing under a licence
  its own NOTICE states.

What this app did to the data (the licence requires this to be stated)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  This app redistributes a **modified, derived** subset of the grammar dataset,
  not the dataset itself. Specifically:

  * Selected all 526 grammar points, with their own example sentences, and nothing
    else — none of the dataset's vocabulary, kanji or levels.
  * **Dropped the level of every point.** The corpus labels each one N5–N1, and
    those labels are not stored, not shown, and not implied: the Japan Foundation
    publishes no official JLPT grammar list, and the app's grammar screen says so.
    The five level files are read as **one course in the order they give**, and
    that order is what the screen offers instead of a level.
  * **Removed the level cross-references inside the notes**, because a note that
    says *"softer requests use 〜てくれませんか (N4)"* would name a level the app does
    not carry. 38 of the 524 notes were changed, each only by losing the label:
    `までに (N4).` became `までに.`, `Formal N2: 〜がたい.` became
    `Formal: 〜がたい.` (a bare label takes the space in front of it with it), and
    *"covered at N3"* became *"covered elsewhere"* — the one construction where
    deleting the label alone would leave a sentence with a hole in it. No other
    wording was touched.
  * Parsed the corpus's own furigana markup (`{私|わたし}は…`) into ruby segments so
    a screen can draw it. **No reading was invented and no alignment was derived**:
    the alignment is the corpus's, and `prepare-grammar` refuses to write an
    example whose markup does not spell its sentence or whose kanji the markup does
    not cover.
  * Recorded, for every example, **which of this app's own words its sentence uses**,
    so the grammar screen can list a pattern's vocabulary and open each word's card.
    That index is not the corpus's and no reading is taken from it: it is UniDic's
    segmentation of the sentence, computed at build time (`prepare-grammar`, whose
    analyser and dictionary are recorded in the artifact and in the analyser notice
    beside this one), and it is what the corpus's own furigana cannot supply — the
    markup marks readings, not word boundaries.
  * Re-encoded the result into a compact binary artifact
    (`crates/nihongo-core/data/grammar.bin.gz`) and recorded OpenJLPT's own version
    in it.

  Those five are changes to the **data**, and they are the whole of them: an
  example sentence is stored, redistributed and shown exactly as the corpus wrote
  it, and the word index beside it is derived from the sentence rather than a
  change to it. The grammar course's first two exercises
  (`nihongo_core::grammar_drill`) draw one of those sentences with the pattern's own
  text blanked at run time — one asks which pattern it is, the other asks the learner
  to write what is missing — and each shows the sentence whole again once an answer is
  given. A **third** stage (`nihongo_core::composition`) shows those sentences
  unaltered, with the corpus's own furigana, as models for a sentence **of the
  learner's own**; that sentence is the learner's writing and is kept in a file of its
  own, and nothing of the corpus's text is changed, blanked or stored differently by
  it. All of that is a presentation of the material rather than a change to it, and it
  is stated here rather than left to a licensor to notice.

Consequence of ShareAlike
-------------------------

  The grammar text stays under CC BY-SA 4.0, and so does `grammar.bin.gz` and
  anything else built from it. This does not reach the program code (AGPL-3.0),
  the kana and kanji geometry (Arphic Public License and LGPL-3.0-or-later), the
  dictionary text (EDRDG, also CC BY-SA 4.0), the alignment (JmdictFurigana, MIT)
  or the app's own interface text.

Keeping it current
------------------

  OpenJLPT rebuilds its dataset monthly and versions it in `data/json/meta.json`.
  The version this build was made from is recorded inside the artifact and asserted
  by `crates/nihongo-core/tests/grammar_artifact.rs`; `scripts/fetch-openjlpt.sh`
  records the URL, size and SHA-256 of every file it fetched in
  `data/raw/openjlpt/PROVENANCE.txt`; and LICENSES.md carries the refresh
  procedure. Refreshing is a deliberate commit — a new release that changes the
  counts fails the artifact test rather than quietly changing the course.
