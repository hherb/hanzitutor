Source
------

  JMdict     https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project
  KANJIDIC2  https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/KANJIDIC_Project
  Licence    https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html

Two forms of each dictionary are read, both from EDRDG's own site or from a pinned
redistribution of its files (see the provenance record in LICENSES.md for the exact
snapshots this build was made from):

  * KANJIDIC2 and JMdict as JSON documents, republished by
    `scriptin/jmdict-simplified` (https://github.com/scriptin/jmdict-simplified),
    a reformatting under the same licence;
  * `JMdict_e.gz`, EDRDG's own XML, read for exactly one field — the `nf01`-`nf48`
    priority rank, which the JSON reformatting drops entirely.

What this app did to the data (the licences require this to be stated)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  This app redistributes a **modified, derived** subset of the dictionary files,
  not the files themselves. Specifically:

  * Selected the 2,136 jōyō kanji and the common vocabulary this course teaches,
    not EDRDG's full dictionary. The kanji set is bounded by the jōyō list; the
    vocabulary is bounded by EDRDG's own "common word" markers and is reduced
    further to the words whose every kanji the course can draw.
  * Kept one reading, one gloss list and one grade per character, and one written
    form, one reading and one gloss list per word. Translational equivalents in
    languages other than English were dropped and are **not** redistributed: EDRDG
    states that those are covered by separate copyright held by their compilers.
  * Composed no reading and invented no reading alignment: a word's reading is the
    dictionary's own kana form, and its furigana comes from JmdictFurigana's
    alignment (see the notice beside this one) rather than from any rule written
    here.
  * Derived a level ladder — the highest kyōiku grade among a word's kanji, with
    EDRDG's frequency rank ordering the words within a band. **The bands are this
    project's approximation and are not the JLPT's**; the app's screens say so.
  * Re-encoded the result into compact binary artifacts
    (`crates/nihongo-core/data/kanji.bin.gz` and `words.bin.gz`) and recorded the
    snapshot's own version and dictionary date in them.

Keeping it current
------------------

  EDRDG's licence states that a package using these files must have a procedure
  for regularly updating them, and that failing to keep them up to date is a
  violation of the licence. The procedure for this app is written down in
  LICENSES.md, section "KANJIDIC2 — CC BY-SA 4.0, with the update obligation
  designed" (and its vocabulary counterpart, "Refreshing it"): the snapshot is
  pinned in `scripts/fetch-data.sh`, the pinned version and date are recorded in
  the artifact and asserted by `crates/nihongo-core/tests/kanji_artifact.rs` and
  `tests/words_artifact.rs`, and a refresh updates the pin, the artifact and those
  tests in one commit.
