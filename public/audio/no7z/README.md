# HSK 1–2 graded sentences — text and audio, and what they are under

This directory holds the app's copy of the **HSK 1–2 graded sentences**: a
`manifest.json` with 819 sentences (Chinese, pinyin, English translation) and the
**corpus's own** recording of each, `<id>.mp3` and `<id>_slow.mp3` — 1,638 clips.

## Upstream, and the attribution it asks for

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Source | <https://huggingface.co/datasets/no7z/hsk-sentences-audio> |
| Text (sentences, pinyin, translations, glosses) | **CC BY-SA 4.0** — <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/> |
| Audio | the corpus's own MP3s, synthesised with **CosyVoice2-0.5B**, redistributed under **Apache-2.0** |

The upstream attribution grants the audio explicitly:

> 音频由 CosyVoice2-0.5B 在本地合成，**合成语音**（synthetic voice）；输入文本为本项目自有内容。
> 本项目依 Apache-2.0 再分发合成输出。

That is: the audio is **synthetic speech**, generated by CosyVoice2-0.5B, and the
upstream project redistributes that output under Apache-2.0. Disclosing it as
synthetic is what the upstream note asks for, and what a learner is entitled to
know. The app's own copy of the full notice is
[`licences/NO7Z-hsk-sentences-audio.txt`](../../../licences/NO7Z-hsk-sentences-audio.txt),
which reproduces the upstream attribution verbatim; the share-alike legal text is
[`licences/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt`](../../../licences/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt).

## Changes made here

* Extracted the HSK 1–2 subset of the sentences into `manifest.json`.
* Used the upstream MP3s **unmodified**, at both speeds — nothing was re-encoded,
  re-voiced or re-timed.
* Renamed them to this app's convention (`<id>.mp3`, `<id>_slow.mp3`) and listed
  them in `manifest.json`, which is a derived index over the files above and adds
  no content of its own.

Because the extracted **text** is an adaptation it stays under CC BY-SA 4.0, as
does `manifest.json`; the **audio** is Apache-2.0 under the upstream grant, which
has no share-alike condition. Neither licence reaches the program code.

## One thing worth knowing before quoting these

The upstream claim that no sentence contains vocabulary above its own level does
not hold: re-grading all of its records against this app's own word list found
about 5% carrying a token above their label. The app treats a level as a rough
band rather than a fact. The research is in
`docs/research/HSK_SENTENCE_SOURCES_LICENSING.md`.
