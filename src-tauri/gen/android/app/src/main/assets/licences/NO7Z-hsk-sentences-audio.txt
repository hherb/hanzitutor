Licence and attribution — HSK-graded sentences with audio
=========================================================

Source
------
no7z/hsk-sentences-audio
https://huggingface.co/datasets/no7z/hsk-sentences-audio

What Hanzi Tutor takes from it
------------------------------
The **text** of the graded sentences: the simplified Chinese, its pinyin, its
English translation, and its per-word glosses. Nothing else.

The **audio is this dataset's own**. Every pronunciation clip the app bundles is
the MP3 the upstream project published for that sentence, at both speeds. The
upstream attribution grants this explicitly:

> 音频由 CosyVoice2-0.5B 在本地合成，**合成语音**（synthetic voice）；输入文本为本项目自有内容。
> 本项目依 Apache-2.0 再分发合成输出。

that is, the audio is synthetic speech generated by CosyVoice2-0.5B, and the
project redistributes that synthesised output under **Apache-2.0**.

**It is disclosed as synthetic speech**, which is what the upstream note asks for
(建议按惯例标注"合成语音") and what a learner is entitled to know: the voice is a
model's, not a person's.

**An earlier revision of this app did not bundle this audio.** It generated its
own with MeloTTS, and then with CosyVoice 3, on the reasoning that one consistent
voice was worth more than the published recordings. Measurement contradicted
that: over 208 HSK-1 phrases, through one recogniser and one judge, MeloTTS failed
57 where these recordings failed 15, leaving 46 phrases that only the generated
audio got wrong. The published audio was better, already in the needed format, and
permissively licensed — so it is what ships. The comparison, and the three
synthesis paths built before it was made, are in
`docs/research/MELOTTS_PRONUNCIATION_ACCURACY.md`.

Licence
-------
Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0)
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/

The upstream project releases its whole `dist/` dataset under CC BY-SA 4.0,
which is why this notice and the licence's full legal text travel with the app.
The sentences and translations are the upstream project's own work; the
per-word glosses and readings derive from CC-CEDICT, itself CC BY-SA 4.0, and
that notice is catalogued separately.

Changes made by Hanzi Tutor
---------------------------
* Extracted a subset of the sentences, filtered to HSK levels 1 and 2.
* Used the upstream MP3s unmodified, at both speeds. Nothing was re-encoded,
  re-voiced or re-timed.
* Renamed them to this app's convention (`<id>.mp3`, `<id>_slow.mp3`) and listed
  them in a `manifest.json`, which is a derived index over the files above and
  adds no content of its own.

**The two licences do not mix.** The sentence text is CC BY-SA 4.0, as
complete-hsk-vocabulary's definitions already are; the audio is Apache-2.0, which
has no share-alike condition. Reusing the recordings does not place the app's code
or its other data under either.
* Wrote the sentences into `public/audio/no7z/manifest.json`, which is a
  derived database of the licence text above and remains under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Because the extracted text is an adaptation, it stays under CC BY-SA 4.0: it may
be reused and redistributed on the same terms, with attribution. The share-alike
condition reaches the text; it does not reach the program code.

**The voice question is now settled by not arising.** The earlier generated
clips cloned a voice from a reference recording, which raised a question this
project could not answer — whose voice, and on what terms. The upstream audio
carries its own voice and its own grant, so there is nothing left to decide and
nothing this notice has to assert on its own authority.

One consequence worth stating: the upstream recordings and the app's on-device
speech (MeloTTS, see its own notice) are **different voices**. That is deliberate
— a phrase with a recording is heard in the course's voice, and a phrase without
one is spoken by the device. They are not meant to match.

A note on the level labels
--------------------------
Upstream grading follows the official 《国际中文教育中文水平等级标准》
(GF0025-2021) and was checked against the community digitisation
`complete-hsk-vocabulary`, which the upstream project itself records as
diverging from the official lists by roughly 1%. Its headline claim that no
sentence contains vocabulary above its own level does not hold: re-grading all
its records against this app's own bundled word list found about 5% carrying a
token above their label. This app therefore does not present the upstream level
as authoritative. See `docs/research/HSK_SENTENCE_SOURCES_LICENSING.md`.
